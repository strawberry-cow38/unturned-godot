# Local player lean

`player/lean.rs` ports the selected Godot `PlayerController.StepLean`, `LeanSpaceEmpty` and `ApplyLean` behavior. The source owns one physical player body; native lean changes its view and pose, not its collision transform or authoritative HP/inventory.

Lean is 20 degrees, positive left/negative right. The requested side is resolved at 50 Hz. Both keys cancel. Sprint, climb, driving, sitting, captured UI and death suppress the request; crouch, prone and swimming remain eligible. A press chooses the third-person shoulder immediately; the shared 0.075 s tap window delays third-person lean so a tap swaps only the shoulder. First-person has no delay. Source keybindings are honored.

Cover uses the original 0.95 m-long capsule with 0.3 m radius, whose complete cap-to-cap extent is strictly `[eye, eye+side*0.95]`. It starts at the unleaned target eye height and checks world/vehicle collision, excluding owner, dropped items and sensors. Rapier's half-segment is 0.175 m because Godot's height includes both caps. A wall flush against the opposite shoulder therefore does not reject leaning away. The selected source deliberately eases an obstructed head back at 4/s, overriding retail's immediate snap; the native port preserves that decision.

The view rolls about a pivot at the feet, moving a standing eye about 0.60 m sideways and slightly downward. Camera and accepted firing origin use the same physical eyes, while the firing basis is body yaw × lean roll × look pitch. Using a generic yaw/pitch/roll Euler constructor would reverse the last two rotations and miss the center while looking up/down around cover. Alt head-look remains independent of the gun's aim. The third-person camera inherits roll, traces from its source upright pivot, and preserves existing eye-plane convergence. Source eye height now eases at 4/s from 1.6 m toward the stance target in both perspectives; its separate third-person pivot eases toward the target once, without double smoothing.

The visible body applies lean to its spine after its real animation/held-item pose. Root yaw and lower-body locomotion stay upright; vehicle/prop-seat branches explicitly clear the spine lean. The native viewmodel's inherited world transform cancels between its camera and arms, so no 3D arm roll is added. Source image roll belongs to the HDR compositor, together with the 1.15×1.45 oversized target and matching source FOV/crop. That companion integration and its visual verification are in progress; no completed render-parity claim is made yet.

The restraint hook sends original `struggle {side:0|1}` only when a nonzero resolved lean side changes and the actual replicated `player.arrested` fact is true. Holding a key, returning upright and an unarrested player produce no request. Missing metadata defaults to unarrested; no client-side cuff strength is invented. The managed appearance publisher is being integrated separately. The original network snapshot does not expose remote-player lean angles; this slice preserves that boundary.

Six focused regressions cover source tap/rules, real Rapier opposite-wall and vehicle obstruction, actual camera peek and pitched aim with Alt separation, UI/pause/session lifetime, actual native fire intent through the managed server, and the real spine/leg/chair pose. The complete player subset passes 13 tests, including the existing seated passenger/furniture gameplay journeys. The authority test spends one real round and checks the accepted event's origin/direction against the physical camera rather than merely testing a shared math helper. Source stair-step camera smoothing, which is independent of stance eye easing, remains outside this slice.

Independent source verification also ran the original Godot 4.6.2 `player.lean` L1 fixture: all 45 checks passed in 15.4 seconds. That dummy-renderer behavior test is not visual evidence. Both root and world source reviewers found no blocking native camera/collision/pose issue.
